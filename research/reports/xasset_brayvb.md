## Interpretation

All four configs pass the pre-registered "robust" rule, but the pass is modest and should be read with the caveats below.

- What it shows: with zero retuning, the default preset earns a positive OOS Sharpe (0.4 to 0.9) on 6 of 7 alts in every config, with a median alt Sharpe of 0.48 to 0.56. FETUSDT is the only negative asset, and it has only 4 folds and 3 to 49 trades, so it is nearly uninformative. The signal is not a BTC-only artefact.
- What it does not show: the strategy rarely beats buy & hold on Sharpe or CAGR (SOL, XRP, BNB and BTC are clearly better held). The Calmar criterion is passed mainly because the strategy sits out the drawdowns of weak alts (ADA, NEAR, FET) where B&H was negative or near zero. The criterion is partly a statement about the bad alts, not about edge. Max drawdowns of 40 to 66% are still large.
- Loses less than B&H: PASS only for 4h (5/7 real, 6/7 ha); FAIL for 1d (2/7 and 1/7), where the strategy is long through most of the drawdowns.
- Statistics: pooled-alts DSR p is 0.016 to 0.076 at N = 191,622 (all below the 0.95 gate by a wide margin). The pooled Sharpe (0.7 to 1.1) does not survive the heavy multiple-testing deflation of the full search. That N is dominated by earlier search trials that are only loosely related to this fixed-preset test, so the DSR is a very conservative reading; with N = 33 it would look far better. Both views are reported honestly; neither is a green light for deployment.
- Real vs HA: HA is better on 4h (pooled Sharpe 1.12 vs 0.92, Calmar wins 6/7 vs 4/7) and slightly better on 1d; HA was disclosed in Amendment 4 as better on BTC 4h, so this is partly the same observation repeated. Per Amendment 4, HA may replace real only if it passes every gate itself, which it does not (DSR).
- Sample caveat: the alts are highly correlated with BTC and with each other (one crypto regime, about 3 years of OOS), so 7 assets are far fewer than 7 independent tests; trade counts on 1d are 8 to 22 per asset.
